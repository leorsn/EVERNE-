# EVERNE — Launch & UGC Plan

## Objective

Validate the Steam Brush as the hero product while using The EVERNE System as the primary AOV upsell. Creative should feel like wardrobe editorial content first and an advertisement second.

## Product hierarchy

1. **Hero product:** Steam Brush — €95
2. **Primary upsell:** The EVERNE System — €129
3. **Supporting products:** Electronic Lint Remover, Cashmere Comb, Fabric Care Brush

The System contains Steam Brush + Electronic Lint Remover + Cashmere Comb. Current individual value: €161. System price: €129. Saving: €32. Moving from Steam Brush to the System costs €34 more.

## Creative rules

- No supplier branding visible.
- Do not imply the products are manufactured by EVERNE.
- No invented testimonials, reviews, certifications or performance statistics.
- Keep backgrounds warm-neutral: cream, stone, wardrobe wood, linen, brushed metal.
- Wardrobe and garment should remain the visual focus; product is the tool.
- Avoid generic dropshipping visual language, hard-sell countdowns, fake scarcity and exaggerated before/after edits.
- Always show real use on suitable garments and follow care labels.

## Core message territories

### 1. Between wears
Position the Steam Brush as the fast wardrobe reset before another wash cycle.

Hooks:
- "Before you wash it again, try this."
- "The two-minute wardrobe reset."
- "What I do before putting a shirt back into rotation."
- "Not dirty. Just not ready to wear again."

### 2. Keep what you already own
Make garment longevity and repeat wear the emotional frame.

Hooks:
- "The best wardrobe upgrade might be taking better care of the clothes you already own."
- "Three tools I use before deciding a garment is finished."
- "A better wardrobe does not always start with buying another piece."

### 3. System vs single tool
Use the €34 step-up from Steam Brush to The EVERNE System as the AOV story.

Hooks:
- "Steam alone or the full wardrobe-care routine?"
- "For €34 more, this is what the full system adds."
- "One tool for creasing. Three tools for the whole surface-care routine."

## UGC concepts

### Concept A — Morning reset
**Format:** 15–20 sec vertical

Shots:
1. Slightly creased shirt on hanger.
2. Close-up of cuff/collar.
3. Steam Brush unfolding.
4. Controlled steaming pass.
5. Garment hanging cleanly.
6. Product on neutral shelf.

Voiceover:
"This shirt is not ready for the laundry. It just needs a reset. I use the Steam Brush between wears to smooth light creasing and get it back into rotation."

End frame:
EVERNE — Care for what you keep.

### Concept B — Three-tool routine
**Format:** 20–30 sec vertical

Shots:
1. Three garments with three different surface problems.
2. Steam Brush on shirt.
3. Electronic Lint Remover on suitable knit/fabric.
4. Cashmere Comb on knitwear.
5. All three tools arranged together.

Voiceover:
"Different wear needs different care. Steam for light creasing, lint removal for visible pilling, and a manual comb for fine knitwear. That is the idea behind The EVERNE System."

End frame:
Steam Brush €95 · System €129

### Concept C — Wardrobe editorial
**Format:** 10–15 sec, music-first

Shots:
- Architectural wardrobe detail.
- Garment texture macro.
- Steam rising in side light.
- Hand folding the Steam Brush.
- Product + garment still life.

On-screen text only:
"Wear longer. Care deliberately. Replace less."

### Concept D — System comparison
**Format:** 15 sec

Frame 1: Steam Brush — €95
Frame 2: + Electronic Lint Remover
Frame 3: + Cashmere Comb
Frame 4: The EVERNE System — €129
Frame 5: Individual value €161 · Save €32

Keep all calculations static and consistent with the current Shopify prices.

## Organic test matrix

Test 3–5 posts per day using combinations of:

- Hook: problem / routine / value / aesthetic
- Opening shot: garment / product / result / wardrobe
- Length: 8–12 sec / 15–20 sec / 25–35 sec
- Audio: voiceover / native sound / restrained trend audio
- CTA: "See the routine" / "Steam or system?" / "Care for what you keep"

Track:

- 3-second hold rate
- average watch time
- completion rate
- saves
- profile visits
- product-page clicks
- Steam Brush vs System click split

Do not promote a creative into paid testing based on views alone. Prioritize retention + click intent.

## Creator brief

### Deliverables per creator

- 3 raw hooks
- 1 complete 15–25 sec edit
- 5–8 clean product-use clips without text
- 2 wardrobe/lifestyle establishing shots
- 1 neutral product still

### Mandatory capture

- Vertical 9:16
- 4K if available
- Natural or soft directional light
- Clean wardrobe / bedroom / dressing-room environment
- No visible third-party logos where avoidable
- No beauty filters that materially change product or garment appearance

### Talking points allowed

- Foldable handheld Steam Brush
- 1000W specification
- Brush attachment and measuring cup included
- Designed for smoothing light creasing and refreshing suitable garments between wears
- System contains Steam Brush, Electronic Lint Remover and Cashmere Comb

### Claims to avoid

- "Sanitizes" or kills bacteria unless independently verified
- Removes odors as a guaranteed performance claim
- Safe for every fabric
- Extends garment life by a quantified amount
- Manufactured or engineered by EVERNE
- Any invented sustainability certification

## Paid testing structure

Only move organic winners into paid testing.

Initial paid test:

- 3 winning creatives
- 3 hook variants per creative
- Steam Brush landing path as control
- System comparison landing path as challenger
- Keep audience structure simple until creative signal is clear

Primary question:
Does the Steam Brush create the strongest first-click intent while The EVERNE System raises checkout value without materially reducing conversion?

## Pre-launch checklist

- Supplier and fulfillment route confirmed
- Product instructions available
- Voltage / plug / compliance information verified for target markets
- Shipping times and return process confirmed
- Final landed cost and contribution margin confirmed
- Purchases remain disabled until the above are cleared
- No test or placeholder domain presented as a final brand domain
